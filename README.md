# First Principles: Political Accountability

## The Core Problem

**Democracy has an information asymmetry problem.**

Politicians:

- Control their narrative (press releases, speeches, media appearances)
- Know citizens have short memories
- Operate in a complex system designed to obscure
- Face consequences only every 4 years, if at all

Citizens:

- Can't track hundreds of votes, motions, speeches
- Forget promises within months
- Don't understand parliamentary procedure
- Have no mechanism to hold anyone accountable between elections

**The result:** Politicians say what wins elections, then do what serves other interests. The gap between words and actions is hidden by complexity and time.

---

## How This Design Addresses It

| Accountability Requirement                  | Current Design                   | Status     |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------- |
| **Transparency** - Actions must be visible  | Shows votes, speeches, motions   | ✅         |
| **Memory** - Promises must be remembered    | Timeline preserves history       | ✅         |
| **Comparison** - Words vs actions           | Contradiction detection          | ✅         |
| **Consequences** - Mechanism for punishment | Shareability → reputation damage | ⚠️ Partial |

**What we've built:** A mirror that reflects what politicians actually do, formatted for virality.

**The theory of change:**

```
Citizens see contradictions
    → Share them
    → Creates social pressure
    → Politicians fear reputation damage
    → More consistency, or electoral loss
    → Democracy works better
```

## Critical Gaps

### 1. The "So What?" Problem

A user sees a contradiction. They share it. Then what?

- No connection to elections
- No call to action beyond sharing
- No mechanism to _do_ anything about it
- Viral outrage fades in 24 hours

**Missing:** A bridge from awareness to action.

### 2. No Citizen Voice

This is a **passive viewing experience**. Citizens can look, but not participate.

- Can't flag issues they care about
- Can't ask politicians questions
- Can't add context or fact-check
- No community layer

**The irony:** A tool to "give power to the people" where the people have no voice.

### 3. No Politician Response

Currently one-sided. Risks being seen as unfair.

- No right of reply
- No space for context ("I voted X because of coalition agreement Y")
- No distinction between hypocrisy and legitimate position evolution
- Could be dismissed as a "gotcha" platform

**Missing:** Fairness that builds legitimacy.

### 4. No Connection to Elections

This should be **most useful** right before people vote. Currently:

- No candidate comparison mode
- No "voter guide" for upcoming elections
- No historical track record summaries
- No integration with campaign promises

**Missing:** The moment when accountability actually matters.

### 5. No Promise Tracking

We catch contradictions _after_ they happen. But:

- Where are the original election promises?
- Where are the party manifestos?
- How do we score promise fulfillment systematically?

**Missing:** The "contract" that politicians made with voters.

### 6. Individual Focus, Systemic Blindness

We focus on individual politicians, but:

- In parliamentary systems, party discipline dominates
- Individual MPs often have no real choice
- The system itself may be the problem
- Lobbying, donors, party structures are invisible

**Missing:** The forces behind individual behavior.

### 7. Context is Absent

We show _what_ they did, not _why_.

- Some votes are strategic
- Coalition politics requires compromise
- Changing position based on new evidence is good
- We might accidentally reward stubbornness over growth

**Risk:** Punishing nuance, rewarding rigid ideology.

## The Bigger Picture

### What Are We Actually Building?

Not just a "politician lookup tool."

**We're building democratic infrastructure.**

The internet disintermediated media, commerce, communication. Democracy is one of the last institutions that hasn't been fundamentally restructured by information technology.

**The vision:**

| Before                                              | After                                           |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| Politicians promise, then disappear into complexity | Every promise is tracked, every vote is visible |
| Citizens engage once every 4 years                  | Continuous, low-friction engagement             |
| Accountability requires journalists to investigate  | Accountability is automatic and crowdsourced    |
| Power asymmetry favors politicians                  | Information parity                              |

### The Ultimate End State

**Politicians behave as if they're always being watched by their constituents. Because they are.**

This isn't surveillance in the authoritarian sense. It's transparency in the democratic sense. Public servants, serving publicly, with public records, made publicly accessible.

## What Would Make This Transformative?

### Phase 1: Awareness (current)

_"See what they did"_

### Phase 2: Voice

_"Tell them what you think"_

- Community flagging of issues
- Questions to politicians
- Upvoting concerns
- Crowdsourced fact-checking

### Phase 3: Action

_"Do something about it"_

- Email your representative (pre-filled)
- Petitions tied to specific actions
- Collective pressure: "4,521 people concerned about this vote"
- Election reminders

### Phase 4: Elections

_"Vote with information"_

- Candidate comparison on your issues
- Promise tracker from manifestos
- Historical consistency scores
- "This is how they voted on issues you care about"

### Phase 5: Systemic

_"See the bigger picture"_

- Party-level analysis
- Lobbying connections
- Donor influence
- Coalition dynamics
- Power structures in committees
